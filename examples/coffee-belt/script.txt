Why can't you plant a coffee tree in your backyard in New York, London, or Paris? After all, it's just a fruit tree, right?
Wrong. Coffee has a fatal biological flaw: zero tolerance for cold. A single night below freezing will destroy an entire plantation.
So why not plant it in the desert? Because coffee also hates intense heat. Temperatures above twenty-four degrees Celsius scorch the leaves and stunt bean growth.
This leaves only one narrow equatorial sanctuary: the Bean Belt, strictly bounded between twenty-five degrees North and twenty-five degrees South.
And inside that belt, Arabica demands mountain highlands, between one thousand and two thousand meters high.
Cool mountain air slows bean maturation, packing in dense sugars, while volcanic soil protects fragile roots. Next time you sip your brew, remember: it survived Earth's narrowest climate tightrope.
